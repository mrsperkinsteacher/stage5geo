Why oil wealth doesn't guarantee wellbeing

📋 Summary of Lesson

This lesson looked at how natural resources, trade and location can improve or limit a country's wellbeing. Transnational corporations can boost a country's GDP, but the profits often go back to the company's home country.

Nigeria and Kuwait both have large oil reserves but very different outcomes: Kuwait has used oil money to fund public services, while in Nigeria the wealth hasn't reached most citizens - an example of the resource curse. Location matters too: landlocked countries like Mongolia, Kazakhstan and Burundi must cross another country's border to reach a port. And Singapore shows the opposite - almost no resources, but a location on one of the world's busiest shipping routes and smart investment in its port, education and finance.

📖 Glossary

Natural resource - materials from nature, like oil, gold or fertile land, that people use or trade.
Landlocked - a country with no direct access to the ocean.
TNC (transnational corporation) - a large company that operates in more than one country.
Resource curse - when a country rich in natural resources ends up with lower wellbeing than expected, often because of corruption or poor management.
Governance - how a country is run - how decisions are made and how money is managed.
Infrastructure - the roads, ports, rail and services a country needs to work and trade.

🗺️ Where in the World?

Singapore - a tiny island on a major shipping route, with very high wellbeing.

Nigeria - one of Africa's largest oil producers, with high poverty rates.

Kuwait - a small oil-rich nation that funds public services, healthcare and education with oil money.

Mongolia and Burundi - landlocked countries that must cross borders to reach a port.
