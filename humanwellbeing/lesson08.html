📋 Summary of Lesson

This lesson showed how environmental factors - air and water pollution - cause variation in wellbeing within a country, using India as the case study. PM2.5 is fine particulate matter small enough to reach deep into the lungs and bloodstream. The Lancet Countdown 2025 linked 1.72 million deaths in India in 2022 to PM2.5 from human activity. In Delhi, PM2.5 takes about 8.2 years off life expectancy; the India average is 3.5 years.

Then the water: the 97 towns on the Ganges' main stem produce about 2.9 billion litres of sewage a day, and in a 2020 survey about 92% of Ganges fishers reported days with no catch at all. You joined the dots from pollution to health, money and school, then evaluated Namami Gange, the government's river clean-up - real progress in places, but only about 63% of its funding spent by March 2025.

📖 Glossary

PM2.5 - fine particulate matter: tiny particles small enough to reach deep into the lungs and bloodstream.
Air Quality Index (AQI) - a scale showing how polluted the air is right now, and its likely health effects.
Water stress - when demand for fresh water is close to, or more than, the reliable supply.
Sanitation - systems that safely manage human waste to protect health and water quality.
Smog - a mix of smoke and fog - air pollution you can see.

🌍 Link to the SDGs

SDG 6 - Clean Water and Sanitation

Sewage in the Ganges is a sanitation problem. This goal is about clean water and safe sanitation for everyone.
SDG 3 - Good Health and Well-being

Air pollution shortens lives. This goal includes reducing deaths from air, water and soil pollution.

The United Nations' 17 Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) are the world's shared plan for improving wellbeing everywhere by 2030.

💡 Did You Know?

PM2.5 caused an estimated 17,188 deaths in Delhi in 2023 alone - nearly 15% of all deaths in the city that year. Across India, the Lancet Countdown 2025 linked 1.72 million deaths in 2022 to PM2.5, up 38% since 2010. How close was your Geo Spark guess?

🗺️ Where in the World?

Delhi - 8.2 years of life expectancy lost to PM2.5 - more than double the India average.

The Ganges - 97 towns on its main stem produce about 2.9 billion litres of sewage a day.

Haridwar to Sultanpur - a stretch of the Ganges now off the polluted list - real progress from Namami Gange.

💼 Careers Connected to This Lesson

Air quality scientist - measures pollution and works out its health effects.

Environmental engineer - designs sewage treatment plants and clean-up projects, like those in Namami Gange.

Public health officer - warns people on bad air days and plans ways to protect the most vulnerable.
