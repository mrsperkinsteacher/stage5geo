📋 Summary of Lesson

This lesson used population profiles (population pyramids) to explain how a country's age structure affects wellbeing. A wide base suggests high birth rates; a wider top suggests more people living to older ages. More children means more demand for schools, and more over-65s means more demand for healthcare.

Yemen and Canada each have about 40 million people, but very different shapes. Yemen has about 16.7 million people under 15 and only about 1.0 million aged 65+. Canada has about 6.0 million under 15 and 7.9 million aged 65+. You calculated age-group percentages from real data and compared the two countries' dependency ratios.

📖 Glossary

Population profile - a graph showing the age and sex structure of a population (also called a population pyramid).
Dependency ratio - the number of people too young or too old to work, compared with the number of working-age people.
Youthful population - a high proportion of children and young people, shown by a wide pyramid base.
Ageing population - a rising share of older people, shown by a wider top on the pyramid.
Fertility rate - the average number of children born per woman.
Demographic transition - the pattern of birth and death rates changing as countries develop.

🌍 Link to the SDGs

SDG 3 - Good Health and Well-being

Youthful populations need child and maternal healthcare; ageing ones need aged care. Both are part of this goal.
SDG 4 - Quality Education

A youthful population like Yemen's needs many more school places right now.

The United Nations' 17 Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) are the world's shared plan for improving wellbeing everywhere by 2030.

💡 Did You Know?

Yemen has about 77 dependants for every 100 working-age people; Canada has about 54. That means each Yemeni worker supports more children and older people than a Canadian worker - even though the two countries have almost the same total population.

🗺️ Where in the World?

Yemen - about 40.6 million people, with a youthful population and a wide pyramid base.

Canada - about 39.7 million people, with an ageing population that relies on immigration to keep its workforce.

Egypt - a wide-based profile, similar to Yemen's.

Japan - almost the opposite shape - one of the oldest populations in the world.
