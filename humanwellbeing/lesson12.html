📋 Summary of Lesson

This lesson looked at how gender inequality in education, work and political voice affects wellbeing. There's real progress: girls now do better than boys globally in school enrolment and completion, and global maternal mortality fell by about 40% between 2000 and 2023.

But big gaps remain. In 2026, 63.9% of women aged 25-54 were in the labour force, compared with 92.0% of men. Maternal deaths are 346 per 100,000 births in low-income countries, but just 10 in high-income countries. Women hold only 27.5% of the world's parliament seats (January 2026), and at the current pace equal representation is still 63 years away. You weighed it all up against SDG 5: real progress, a stalled effort, or both?

📖 Glossary

Gender gap - the difference between men's and women's access to opportunities, resources or power.
Labour force participation - the share of working-age people who are employed or looking for work.
Political representation - the share of decision-making roles, like parliament seats, held by a group.
Maternal mortality - deaths of women from pregnancy or childbirth causes, per 100,000 births.
SDG 5 - the UN goal to achieve gender equality by 2030.

💡 Did You Know?

Women hold about 27 in every 100 parliament seats worldwide (27.5%, January 2026). How close was your Geo Spark guess? It varies hugely by region: from 16.2% in the Middle East and North Africa to 35.6% in the Americas.
