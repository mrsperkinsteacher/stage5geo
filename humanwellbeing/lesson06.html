📋 Summary of Lesson

This lesson used Syria as a case study of how political conflict changes wellbeing. Syria's HDI is 0.564 (2023), classed 'medium', compared with Australia's 0.958. During the war, Syria's wellbeing went backwards while most countries kept improving. Life expectancy fell from about 73.5 years (2010) to a low of about 63 (2015), then recovered to about 72 by 2023.

You looked at displacement: about 5.5 million people are still displaced inside Syria and about 4.7 million are refugees in the region (March 2026). After the government of Bashar al-Assad fell in December 2024, more than 1.5 million refugees returned and 1.8 million internally displaced people went home. You saw how wellbeing varies within Syria too, and evaluated the World Food Programme's response - real help, but cut in half when funding ran out.

📖 Glossary

Displacement - being forced to leave home because of conflict, either within your own country or across a border.
IDP (internally displaced person) - someone forced from home who still remains inside their own country.
Refugee - someone forced to flee across a border into another country.
Humanitarian assistance - aid that meets people's basic needs - food, water, shelter, healthcare - during or after a crisis.
Transitional government - a government put in place temporarily after major political change.
Reconstruction - rebuilding infrastructure, services and the economy after conflict.

🗺️ Where in the World?

Aleppo - during the fighting in late 2024, most private hospitals closed; in nearby rural areas no public hospitals or clinics were working at all.

North-east Syria - damage to the Tishreen Dam disrupted water and electricity for more than 413,000 people in 2024-25.

Türkiye - hosts about 2.87 million of the 4.7 million registered Syrian refugees in the region.

💼 Careers Connected to This Lesson

Humanitarian aid worker - delivers food, water and shelter in a crisis, with organisations like the World Food Programme.

Refugee caseworker - helps displaced families find safety, services and a way home or a new home.

Reconstruction engineer - rebuilds roads, water systems, schools and hospitals after conflict.
