📋 Summary of Lesson

This lesson opens the Human Wellbeing unit. You worked out what "wellbeing" actually means as a geographer, not just a feel-good word: wellbeing is a person or community's quality of life, shaped by material things (income, housing, food) and non-material things (health, safety, freedom, relationships).

You met the five domains geographers use to break wellbeing down - STEEP (Social, Technological, Economic, Environmental, Political) - and looked at three different perspectives on what "a good life" means: a Western/economic view (focused on income and consumption), a wellbeing/happiness view (focused on life satisfaction), and a Biblical perspective (focused on shalom - wholeness, right relationship with God, others and creation, not just material comfort).

You finished by previewing the OECD's Better Life Index as a real tool that tries to measure wellbeing across many of these domains at once.

📖 Glossary

Wellbeing - a person or community's overall quality of life - material and non-material.
Material wellbeing - quality of life measured by physical, measurable things - income, housing, food, healthcare access.
Non-material wellbeing - quality of life measured by things that can't be bought - safety, freedom, relationships, sense of purpose.
STEEP - a framework for sorting factors affecting wellbeing into Social, Technological, Economic, Environmental and Political categories.
Shalom - a Biblical concept of wellbeing as wholeness and right relationship - with God, other people, and creation - not just material comfort.

💡 Did You Know?

The OECD's Better Life Index lets you build your own personal definition of a good life by weighting 11 different topics (from income to life satisfaction) however you think matters most - and different countries top the list depending on which topics you weight highest. There's no single "correct" way to measure wellbeing, which is exactly why geographers use several measures side by side rather than just one.

🗺️ Where in the World?

Finland - has topped the World Happiness Report's global ranking for several years running, credited to strong social trust, healthcare and work-life balance rather than the highest income in the world.

Bhutan - famously measures Gross National Happiness (GNH) instead of GDP as its main measure of national progress - one of the first countries to formally do this.
