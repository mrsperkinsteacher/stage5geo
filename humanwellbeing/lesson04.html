📋 Summary of Lesson

This lesson looked at life expectancy - the average number of years a person is expected to live in a given country. It's one of the most widely used wellbeing indicators because it reflects a whole population's health, nutrition and living conditions, and it's one of the three parts of the HDI.

Global life expectancy rose from around 51 years in 1960 to around 73 years in 2023, thanks to better health care, sanitation, vaccination and access to food and clean water. But the rise isn't even: Japan and Australia average above 83 years, while Nigeria and Chad are around 55. Gaps exist inside countries too - for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples, life expectancy is 71.9 years for males and 75.6 for females (ABS, 2020-2022), below the national average. You finished by comparing two countries with similar incomes but different life expectancies on Gapminder.

📖 Glossary

Life expectancy - the average number of years a person is expected to live in a given country.
Disparity - a noticeable difference or gap between groups or places.
HDI (Human Development Index) - a measure built from life expectancy, education and income combined.
Diminishing returns - when each extra dollar spent adds less than before - why rich countries' life expectancy is rising more slowly.
Double burden of malnutrition - undernutrition and obesity existing side by side, often in the same country.

🗺️ Where in the World?

Japan - about 84 years - one of the highest life expectancies in the world.

Chad and Nigeria - around 55 years, at the other end of the scale.

Australia - above 83 years on average, but a gap remains for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples.

Portugal and Russia - the pair in your SUPPORT box - check them on Gapminder to see how income and health don't always move together.

💼 Careers Connected to This Lesson

Epidemiologist - studies the patterns and causes of health and disease in populations - the work behind life expectancy figures.

Public health researcher - works out why health outcomes differ between and within countries, and which policies actually help.

Aboriginal health worker - provides health care and support in community, helping close the gap you looked at today.
