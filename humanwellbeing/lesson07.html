📋 Summary of Lesson

This lesson explained the causes of spatial variation in wellbeing using five lenses - the STEEP idea from Lesson 01, with Historic instead of Technological: Environmental (like the 2011 tsunami), Social, Economic, Historic and Political.

Japan was the case study. Tokyo has a huge economy - about US$63,000 per person (FY2022) - and over 3.3 doctors per 1000 people. Rural Japan has about 2 doctors per 1000 people, more than 6000 rural schools have closed since 2000, and Akita has lost more than a quarter of its population since 1980. Japan's population is ageing and shrinking: about 30% of people are 65 or older, and the fertility rate hit a record low of 1.14 in 2025. Some villages are now genkai shuraku - places with too few people left to keep basic services going.

📖 Glossary

Spatial variation - the way something, like wellbeing, differs from place to place.
Urbanisation - the growth in the share of people living in cities rather than rural areas.
Ageing population - a population with a rising share of older people and a falling share of children.
Brain drain - when skilled or educated people leave a region for better opportunities elsewhere.
Genkai shuraku - 'marginal settlements' - Japanese villages with too few residents left to sustain basic services.
Karoshi - a Japanese term meaning death caused by overwork.
