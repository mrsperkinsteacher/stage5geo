📋 Summary of Lesson

This lesson taught you to read a choropleth map - a map that uses different shades of the same colour to show a pattern in data. Darker usually means a higher value, lighter a lower one, and the legend tells you exactly what each shade means.

You used the PQE method - Pattern, Quantify, Exception - on a real Human Development Index (HDI) map. Very high HDI clusters in Western Europe, North America, East Asia and Australasia; low HDI clusters in Sub-Saharan Africa and in places affected by conflict, such as Afghanistan and Yemen. Then you built your own map from a 30-country life satisfaction data sheet (World Happiness Report 2026) and wrote your own PQE.

📖 Glossary

Choropleth map - a map that uses different shades of the same colour to show a geographic pattern in data.
PQE - Pattern, Quantify, Exception - the three-step method for analysing any map or data set.
Spatial pattern - how something is distributed across space - where it clusters and where it's sparse.
Legend - the key on a map that tells you exactly what each shade or symbol means.
HDI (Human Development Index) - a UN measure combining life expectancy, education and income into one score from 0 to 1.
Life satisfaction - how people rate their own life, from 0 (worst possible life) to 10 (best possible life).
Cartogram - a map that resizes each country by the data, not by its land area.

💡 Did You Know?

Seychelles has an HDI of 0.848, classed 'very high' - far above most of Sub-Saharan Africa. That makes it a real exception on the map. An exception isn't a mistake in the data; it's often the most interesting thing on the map, because it tells you something else is going on.

🗺️ Where in the World?

Iceland - the highest HDI in the world at 0.972 (2023 data).

Australia - an HDI of 0.958, in the 'very high' group.

South Sudan - the lowest HDI in the world at 0.388.

Seychelles - 0.848, 'very high' - the exception that breaks the Sub-Saharan Africa pattern.
