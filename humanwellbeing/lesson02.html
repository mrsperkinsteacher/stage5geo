📋 Summary of Lesson

This lesson taught you how to read a choropleth map - a map that uses colour shading to show how a value (like the Human Development Index) varies from place to place - using the PQE method: Pattern (what's the overall trend?), Quantify (use real numbers from the map/legend), Exception (what breaks the pattern, and why?).

You applied PQE to a real HDI world map, then practised it again with a genuine 30-country life satisfaction dataset, mapping and describing the data yourselves. Along the way you met the Human Development Index itself - a UN measure combining income, education and life expectancy into a single score - as a second way (alongside the World Happiness Report) that wellbeing gets measured globally.

📖 Glossary

Choropleth map - a map that uses colour shading to represent how much of a value exists in each area.
PQE method - Pattern, Quantify, Exception - a structured way to describe and analyse a choropleth map.
Human Development Index (HDI) - a UN measure combining income, education and life expectancy into a single score from 0 to 1.
Life satisfaction - a self-reported measure of how happy or content people say they are with their lives, usually on a 0-10 scale.

🌍 Link to the SDGs

SDG 10 - Reduced Inequalities

SDG 17 - Partnerships for the Goals

reliable global data like the HDI only exists because countries and the UN work together to collect and share it.

The United Nations' 17 Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) are the world's shared framework for improving wellbeing globally by 2030.

💡 Did You Know?

Norway has ranked among the very top countries on the Human Development Index for years, but it does not always top the World Happiness Report in the same year - a genuine example of an "exception" a geographer would flag using the PQE method, since two different, both legitimate, measures of wellbeing don't always agree on which country is "best off".

🗺️ Where in the World?

Norway - consistently ranks in the top handful of countries on the HDI, driven by high income, strong education access and long life expectancy.

Niger - has consistently ranked among the lowest HDI scores globally, reflecting major gaps in income, education access and life expectancy - a useful real contrast when mapping global patterns.
