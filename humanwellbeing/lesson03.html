📋 Summary of Lesson

This lesson unpacked the difference between GDP (Gross Domestic Product - the value of everything produced inside a country's borders) and GNI (Gross National Income - the income earned by a country's citizens and businesses, wherever in the world they earn it).

You looked at how transnational corporations (TNCs) complicate GDP figures, then worked through three real case studies: Switzerland's MONET 2030 wellbeing-measurement system, Australia's own Measuring What Matters framework, and the puzzle of why tiny, oil-rich Qatar has one of the highest GDP-per-capita figures in the world despite a small population. You finished by looking at what GDP and GNI leave out altogether - inequality, environmental cost, unpaid work - and why "beyond GDP" measures matter.

📖 Glossary

GDP (Gross Domestic Product) - the total value of everything produced inside a country's borders in a year.
GNI (Gross National Income) - the total income earned by a country's citizens and businesses, wherever in the world they earn it.
Transnational corporation (TNC) - a company that operates in more than one country - its profits can complicate a single country's GDP figures.
GDP per capita - GDP divided by population - used to compare average economic output between countries of very different sizes.
Beyond GDP - the general push to measure national progress using wellbeing indicators as well as, or instead of, GDP alone.

💡 Did You Know?

Qatar has one of the highest GDP-per-capita figures in the world - but this comes from dividing a huge oil-and-gas-driven GDP by a very small citizen population, much of which is made up of foreign workers whose earnings are counted in Qatar's GDP but often sent home as GNI to another country entirely. It is a real, concrete example of why GDP per capita alone can be a misleading measure of an average person's actual wellbeing.

🗺️ Where in the World?

Switzerland - uses its MONET 2030 system to track wellbeing alongside GDP, spanning environmental, social and economic indicators.

Qatar - has extremely high GDP per capita driven by oil and gas exports and a small population - a real case study in why GDP per capita can mislead.

Australia - reports Measuring What Matters annually alongside GDP, covering five wellbeing themes: healthy, secure, sustainable, cohesive and prosperous.
