📋 Summary of Lesson

This lesson compared the two main measures of a country's wealth. GDP (Gross Domestic Product) asks: where was it made? It counts everything produced inside a country's borders. GNI (Gross National Income) asks: who earned it? It counts what a country's own residents and businesses earn, wherever they earn it. You tested this with Sam Kerr's prize money and a South Korean car factory in Australia.

You saw how transnational corporations like Nike add to a country's GDP while the profit flows back home, and why neither GDP nor GNI shows pollution, stress, or how evenly wealth is shared - look at Rio de Janeiro, where favelas sit right next to wealthy neighbourhoods. Then you looked at countries measuring more than GDP: Switzerland's MONET 2030 and Australia's Measuring What Matters. Finally, you found out why Qatar's GDP per person is so high.

📖 Glossary

GDP (Gross Domestic Product) - the value of all goods and services produced within a country's borders, no matter who produced them.
GNI (Gross National Income) - the value of what a country's own residents and businesses earn, no matter where in the world they earned it.
TNC (transnational corporation) - a company, like Nike, that operates and sells in many countries but sends profits back to its home country.
GDP per capita - GDP divided by population - used to compare countries of very different sizes.
Beyond GDP - measuring a country's progress with more than one economic number, as MONET 2030 and Measuring What Matters do.

💡 Did You Know?

Qatar has one of the highest GDP per capita figures in the world, and it's real, not a statistical trick. Two reasons: a very small population, and extremely valuable natural gas and oil exports. Because GDP per capita divides by population, a small population with high export income pushes the figure up sharply.

🗺️ Where in the World?

Switzerland - tracks progress with MONET 2030 - over 100 environmental, social and economic indicators, including the poverty rate and gender wage gap.

Australia - uses Measuring What Matters (2023), 50 indicators across five themes.

Qatar - very high GDP per capita from a small population and gas and oil exports.

Rio de Janeiro, Brazil - favelas next to wealthy neighbourhoods - one city, two very different lives.
