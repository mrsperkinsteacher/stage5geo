Task overview

You will choose a local environmental issue and complete an ‘Environmental Management Plan’ (EMP) for this issue.

‘Local’ means within the Central Coast (Gosford Council) area — if you want to use somewhere outside this area (e.g. Northern Beaches, Newcastle), you must get permission first, as it affects the Aboriginal Custodianship of Country section. You are to be visiting the site you choose more than once for primary data collection, so local areas are usually best.

The EMP can be a broad overview of the best way to manage a particular location, or a topical study on the most effective option given a range of possible solutions for an area. For example, your EMP could provide a broad conclusion for the best way to manage coastal erosion at Shelly Beach, or investigate whether a seawall is the most effective option to protect the Entrance Inlet.

Primary data is information you collect yourself (e.g. surveys, written observations, measurements, photographs). Secondary data is information already presented in someone else’s research (e.g. ABS data, government reports, Council EMPs). Both are required — see the Findings section below.

Structure of the report — 55 marks

Each section below shows what to include and the full marking criteria (A–E) used to grade it. Paragraph counts are a guide only, to help you not ‘underwrite’ a section.

5 MARKS

1. Inquiry Question (Title)

Criterion: Contextual understanding

What to do

Choose a key inquiry question for the report (e.g. ‘To what extent is wetland restoration the most effective way to manage flooding at Wattlebrook Creek?’).

Marking criteria

BandMarksDescriptor
A (Extensive)5Poses a clear, geographically specific inquiry question that names the issue, the location and an evaluative focus.
B (Thorough)4Poses a well-focused inquiry question that names the issue and location with a mostly clear evaluative focus.
C (Sound)3Poses an appropriate inquiry question that names the issue and location but is descriptive rather than evaluative.
D (Basic)2Poses a basic inquiry question that names the issue but lacks a clear location and/or evaluative focus.
E (Elementary)1Makes an attempt at an inquiry question, but it is vague, non-geographical, or does not match the report that follows.
5 MARKS

2. Background to the Issue

Criterion: Change over time and sustainability

What to do

  1. Identify the issue and describe how it has developed and why, with specific reference to change over time (1–2 paragraphs).
  2. Describe the site (3–4 sentences) — e.g. its scale, land use and key features.
  3. Prepare a labelled location map (bird’s eye view screenshot from Google Maps) using BOLTSS conventions (border, orientation/north point, legend, title, scale, source).

Marking criteria

BandMarksDescriptor
A (Extensive)5Explains, with specific and accurate dates/data, how the issue has changed over time and why; precisely describes the site (scale, land use, key features); includes an accurately labelled bird’s eye view map (BOLTSS conventions).
B (Thorough)4Explains, with mostly specific and accurate detail, how the issue has changed over time; detailed description of the site; includes a bird’s eye view map with only minor labelling gaps.
C (Sound)3Describes, in general terms, how the issue has developed over time; adequately describes the site; includes a bird’s eye view map missing one labelling element.
D (Basic)2States, with limited explanation, how the issue developed; basic or generic site description; map present but missing several labelling elements.
E (Elementary)1Makes a limited attempt to identify the issue and/or describe the site; explanation of development is minimal or absent; map, if included, is unlabelled or not a bird’s eye view.
15 MARKS

3. Findings — Primary and Secondary Data

Criterion: Processing geographical information

What to do

  1. Primary data (e.g. your collected survey results, measurements, field observations, your own photographs): analyse the information, identifying patterns, trends and interconnections, and cite specific figures/data as evidence (2–3 paragraphs).
  2. Secondary data (e.g. council reports, ABS/government data, existing studies, flood/hazard maps or quotes from reputable sources): analyse the information, identifying patterns, trends and interconnections, and cite specific figures/data as evidence (2–3 paragraphs).
Note: a report missing primary data, or missing secondary data, cannot be assessed above a D in this section — both are required.

Marking criteria

BandMarksDescriptor
A (Extensive)13–15Includes multiple primary AND secondary data sources (a genuine range of types — e.g. survey results, measurements, photographs, council/government data), clearly relevant and correctly labelled, with sustained analysis that explicitly identifies patterns, trends and interconnections and consistently cites specific, accurate figures/data as evidence.
B (Thorough)10–12Includes several primary AND secondary data sources, relevant and appropriately labelled. Analysis refers consistently to the data and identifies at least one pattern or trend in each set.
C (Sound)6–9Includes at least one primary AND one secondary data source relevant to the inquiry question; presentation is inconsistent. Analysis makes general reference to the data without close detail.
D (Basic)3–5Includes data from only one of primary or secondary sources (not both), OR data present but lacking clear labelling or relevance. Analysis largely describes rather than analyses the data.
E (Elementary)1–2Primary and/or secondary data is attempted but very limited, missing, or largely irrelevant; analysis is minimal and rarely refers to the data.
5 MARKS

4. Aboriginal Custodianship of Country

Criterion: Aboriginal Custodianship of Country

What to do

  1. Identify one example of Aboriginal Custodianship of Country connected to your issue or site (a practice, piece of knowledge, or a Traditional Owner group’s own statement).
  2. Explain how this example supports sustainable management and Community wellbeing for your site (1–2 paragraphs).
  3. Use at least one credible source: a Traditional Owner group’s own materials, your Local Aboriginal Land Council (Darkinjung LALC for the Central Coast), AIATSIS, or NSW Government Aboriginal affairs resources. Cite accurately — the LALC speaks for the community, it is not a Traditional Owner’s own statement.
You may draw on this research again in your Future Solutions section if a Custodianship-informed approach is one of your management options.

Marking criteria

BandMarksDescriptor
A (Extensive)5Identifies a specific example of Custodianship (named practice, knowledge, or Traditional Owner statement) and explains its link to both sustainable management and Community wellbeing for the site, with an accurate credible source.
B (Thorough)4Identifies a specific example of Custodianship and explains its link to management or wellbeing for the site, with an accurate credible source.
C (Sound)3Identifies an example of Custodianship and describes, in general terms, its link to management or wellbeing, with a credible source.
D (Basic)2Identifies a superficial or generic example of Custodianship, with an unclear link to management or wellbeing for the site.
E (Elementary)1Makes a minimal or unrelated attempt to reference Custodianship.
10 MARKS

5. Future Solutions

Criterion: Evaluation and judgement

What to do

  1. Identify and describe possible solutions for managing the issue, aiming for at least three (1–2 paragraphs).
  2. Determine the most effective option based on the criteria below (approx. 3 paragraphs):
    • Economic viability — is it affordable?
    • Social justice — is it fair to all the people involved?
    • Environmental benefit — is it sustainable and has minimal environmental impact?

Marking criteria

BandMarksDescriptor
A (Extensive)9–10Identifies at least three realistic, specific management options using precise geographical terminology. Assesses the preferred option against all three criteria (economic viability, social justice, environmental benefit) with sustained, well-reasoned judgements supported by evidence from the Findings.
B (Thorough)7–8Identifies at least three suitable options using relevant terminology. Assesses the preferred option against the three criteria with thorough reasoning, referring to the Findings.
C (Sound)5–6Identifies at least two suitable options using relevant terminology. Assesses the preferred option against the three criteria with general reasoning that does not draw specifically on the Findings.
D (Basic)2–4Identifies one or two options using basic terminology. Assessment against the criteria is attempted but generic or only partially addresses each one.
E (Elementary)1Attempts to identify a management option, but it is unsuitable, unrealistic or not clearly linked to the issue; assessment against the criteria is minimal or absent.
5 MARKS

6. Conclusion

Criterion: Explanation and reasoning

What to do

Provide a short summary of your EMP which conclusively identifies and justifies the most appropriate action from the listed future solutions (1 paragraph).

Marking criteria

BandMarksDescriptor
A (Extensive)5Clearly identifies a single preferred action, with justification that draws specifically on both the Findings and the evaluation criteria.
B (Thorough)4Clearly identifies a single preferred action, with justification that draws on either the Findings or the evaluation criteria.
C (Sound)3Identifies a preferred action, with a general justification that does not draw specifically on the Findings or evaluation criteria.
D (Basic)2Identifies a preferred action, but with minimal or unclear justification.
E (Elementary)1Does not clearly identify a single preferred action, or the conclusion does not relate to the rest of the report.
5 MARKS

7. Referencing

Criterion: Referencing and use of sources

What to do

  1. Include in-text citations for every source used throughout your report.
  2. Provide a reference list using APA 7th referencing, with each in-text citation matching an entry in the list.
Fabricated, non-existent, or unverifiable sources cannot be credited, regardless of formatting accuracy.

Marking criteria

BandMarksDescriptor
A (Extensive)5All sources, including any AI tool used, are referenced accurately and consistently using APA, with every in-text citation matching an entry in the reference list.
B (Thorough)4Almost all sources are referenced using APA, with in-text citations matching the reference list for all but 1–2 sources; AI use, if any, is referenced.
C (Sound)3Most sources are referenced using APA, but 3 or more in-text citations are missing, mismatched, or don’t correspond to a reference-list entry; AI use, if any, is referenced.
D (Basic)2Some sources are referenced using APA, but inconsistently or incompletely, and conventions are only partially applied.
E (Elementary)1Makes a minimal attempt at referencing; sources listed are incomplete, inconsistent, or do not follow APA conventions. Limited or no in-text citation.
5 MARKS

8. Communication

Criterion: Editing, grammar and spelling

What to do

Marked throughout the report for accurate spelling, grammar, punctuation and consistent use of geographical terminology.

Marking criteria

BandMarksDescriptor
A (Extensive)5Writing is fluent and precise, with accurate spelling, grammar and punctuation throughout, and consistent use of geographical terminology and a formal tone throughout.
B (Thorough)4Writing is fluent with mostly accurate spelling, grammar and punctuation, and consistent use of geographical terminology and a mostly formal tone.
C (Sound)3Writing is generally clear with mostly accurate spelling and grammar; occasional errors do not interrupt meaning; geographical terminology and tone are mostly appropriate for a formal report.
D (Basic)2Writing is understandable but spelling, grammar or punctuation errors occur regularly; geographical terminology is used inconsistently.
E (Elementary)1Writing shows an attempt at communication, but frequent spelling, grammar or punctuation errors interrupt meaning; geographical terminology is rarely used and the tone is inconsistent or informal.
